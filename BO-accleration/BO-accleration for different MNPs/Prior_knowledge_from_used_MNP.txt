Non-linear patterns (based on the six variables)

- ion_concentration_in_water: Bell-shaped response. Performance peaks at an intermediate window (about 1.0–1.5), where images show uniform aggregates in oil with minimal residue and satellites. Both lower (<1.0) and higher (>1.5) concentrations degrade outcomes, evidenced by increased residue/satellites or loss of uniformity despite interface crossing.

- ratio_of_surfactant_in_water: Threshold then plateau. Zero concentration is consistently detrimental even when crossing occurs (residue persists). Introducing a small, nonzero amount (0–0.1) produces a marked improvement (cleaner crossings, better uniformity), while further increases into 0.1–0.5 yield only modest additional gains.

- ratio_of_surfactant_in_oil: Step-change with mid-range benefit. Absence of surfactant is harmful (residue and/or non-uniform clusters). Small additions (0–0.1) noticeably improve transfer quality, and moving to a moderate range (0.1–0.5) tends to provide the cleanest crossings with good uniformity, indicating a non-linear jump rather than a gradual linear trend.

- oil_type: Strong categorical discontinuities. One oil (7500) consistently yields superior outcomes with clean, uniform clusters; PMX-20 is also beneficial. In contrast, several others (e.g., FC-40, PFPE, PMX-200, PMX-350, mineral) systematically hinder clean transfer (extensive residue or higher satellite fractions). Differences within the silicone family (PMX-20 vs PMX-200/350) underscore non-linear category effects rather than any simple ordering.
